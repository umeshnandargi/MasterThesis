\section{Motivation}
Robotic manipulation plays a crucial role in modern automated production
systems, particularly in logistics, assembly, and bin-picking applications. A
fundamental component of such systems is the gripper, which enables robots to
interact with objects in their environment. Among the most widely used gripping
technologies are suction-cup grippers and two-finger mechanical grippers, both
of which offer distinct advantages depending on the object geometry, material
properties, and task constraints.

Suction-cup grippers are commonly used for handling flat or smooth surfaces and
offer fast, simple gripping mechanisms with minimal mechanical complexity. In
contrast, two-finger grippers provide greater flexibility when handling objects
with irregular shapes or when force-controlled grasping is required. However,
selecting optimal grasp parameters and configurations for these grippers is a
complex problem due to the large number of influencing factors such as object
geometry, grasp stability, contact forces, and collision constraints.
Traditional design and tuning approaches often rely on heuristic rules or manual
parameter adjustment. These methods can be time-consuming and may not produce
optimal results, particularly in environments where multiple performance
criteria must be satisfied simultaneously. In practical robotic systems, several
objectives must be considered at once, such as maximizing grasp stability,
minimizing collision risk, improving grasp success rate, and ensuring efficient
robot motion.

To address these challenges, multi-objective optimization techniques provide a
systematic way to evaluate and optimize multiple competing objectives
simultaneously. By exploring the design space efficiently, modern optimization
algorithms can identify high-quality grasp configurations that balance
performance trade-offs.

In particular, algorithms such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Bayesian
optimization methods, and Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs) have shown strong
potential in solving complex optimization problems in robotics due to their
ability to handle nonlinear, high-dimensional, and non-convex search spaces.

Therefore, applying multi-objective optimization techniques to the design and
parameterization of suction-cup and two-finger grippers can significantly
improve grasp planning and robotic manipulation performance.

\chapter{Goal and Task of the Thesis}

The goal of this thesis is to develop and evaluate a multi-objective
optimization framework for determining optimal grasp configurations for
suction-cup and two-finger robotic grippers.

The thesis aims to investigate how different optimization algorithms can be
applied to improve grasp performance by simultaneously considering multiple
relevant objectives. These objectives may include grasp stability, force
distribution, collision avoidance, and grasp feasibility.

To achieve this goal, the following tasks are defined:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Literature Review}

    Study existing research on robotic grasping, gripper design, and
    multi-objective optimization techniques used in robotic manipulation.

    \item \textbf{Modeling of Grasping Systems}

    Define appropriate models for suction-cup and two-finger grippers, including
    the relevant parameters influencing grasp quality.

    \item \textbf{Definition of Optimization Objectives}

    Identify and formulate multiple objective functions that characterize the
    quality of a grasp.

    \item \textbf{Implementation of Optimization Algorithms}

    Implement and apply different optimization algorithms such as Particle Swarm
    Optimization, Bayesian-based optimization methods, and Evolutionary
    Algorithms.

    \item \textbf{Evaluation and Comparison}

    Analyze and compare the performance of the applied algorithms with respect
    to convergence behavior, computational efficiency, and resulting grasp
    quality.

    \item \textbf{Result Analysis}

    Evaluate the trade-offs between objectives and determine suitable grasp
    configurations for both gripper types.
\end{enumerate}

Through these tasks, the thesis aims to provide insights into how
multi-objective optimization can enhance robotic grasp planning and contribute
to more efficient and reliable robotic manipulation systems.

\chapter{Approach}

To address the research objectives, a systematic optimization framework is
developed for evaluating grasp configurations of suction-cup and two-finger
grippers.

First, the grasping problem is formulated as a \textbf{multi-objective
optimization problem}, where several criteria relevant to grasp quality are
defined as objective functions. These functions may include metrics related to
grasp stability, contact forces, collision avoidance, and geometric feasibility.
The optimization variables correspond to parameters such as grasp positions,
orientations, and gripper configurations.

Next, three different classes of optimization algorithms are applied to explore
the solution space:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO)}, which is a population-based metaheuristic inspired by collective behavior in nature.

    \item \textbf{Bayesian-based optimization methods}, which use probabilistic models to efficiently guide the search toward promising regions of the solution space.

    \item \textbf{Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs)}, which simulate natural selection processes to iteratively improve candidate solutions.
\end{itemize}

Each algorithm generates a set of candidate grasp configurations and iteratively
refines them based on the defined objective functions. Because multiple
objectives are considered simultaneously, the algorithms produce a set of
\textbf{Pareto-optimal solutions}, representing different trade-offs between
competing objectives.

The performance of the algorithms is then evaluated through simulation
experiments. Metrics such as convergence behavior, computational efficiency, and
quality of the resulting Pareto fronts are analyzed to determine the
effectiveness of each optimization approach.

Finally, the obtained results are compared for suction-cup and two-finger
grippers to identify optimal grasping strategies and to evaluate how different
optimization techniques influence grasp performance.
