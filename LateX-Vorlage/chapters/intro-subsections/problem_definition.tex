\section{Problem Definition}
\label{sec:goals_tasks}

The design of task-specific robotic grippers remains largely a manual and experience-driven process. Geometric parameters, such as the placement of
suction cups or the contour of gripper jaws, are commonly selected iteratively and subsequently validated through simulation, geometric evaluation,
or physical experiments \cite{Liu2022Simulation}. This procedure can become time-consuming when many possible configurations must be considered.

For the gripper-design problems investigated in this thesis, the performance of a candidate configuration is determined by computational evaluation
routines. These routines do not provide analytical gradients or closed-form expressions that directly relate the design parameters to the resulting
performance. Furthermore, a gripper design must generally satisfy several criteria simultaneously, for example by performing across multiple
workpieces, grasp locations, or geometric requirements. Improvements with respect to one criterion may reduce performance with respect to another.
Representing all criteria by a single aggregated value could hide these trade-offs.

These characteristics lead to expensive, derivative-free, multi-objective black-box optimization problems \cite{Mueller2017SOCEMO}. A variety of
optimization approaches can be applied to such problems, including evolutionary multi-objective algorithms and Bayesian optimization methods.
However, their performance depends on factors such as the problem representation, number of objectives, objective landscape, and available
evaluation budget \cite{Santoni2023Comparison,Raponi2023Optimizing}. Consequently, results obtained for generic test problems or other applications
cannot necessarily be transferred directly to task-specific gripper design.

The primary goal of this thesis is therefore to apply and comparatively evaluate selected evolutionary and Bayesian multi-objective optimization
methods for automated gripper configuration. Two application cases are considered: the
configuration of a multi-suction-cup gripper and the optimization of rigid jaw contours for a two-finger gripper. In the first case, the positions of
the suction cups are optimized to obtain layouts that perform across a given set of workpieces. In the second case, spline-based jaw contours are
optimized to provide suitable geometric conformity at predefined grasp points. The two cases provide different geometric representations and
objective structures through which the behaviour of the selected optimization algorithms can be investigated.

The objective is not to identify a universally superior optimization algorithm. Instead, the thesis examines how the selected methods perform on the
two specific gripper-design problems under comparable experimental conditions. The comparison considers the quality of the obtained
multi-objective solutions, their development over the available evaluation budget, and the consistency of the results across repeated runs.

To achieve this goal, the following tasks are defined:

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Problem formulation:}
    Formulate the multi-suction and two-finger gripper configuration problems as multi-objective black-box optimization tasks by specifying their
    decision variables, objectives, and applicable constraints.

    \item \textbf{Framework integration:}
    Establish a common optimization interface through which the selected algorithms can access both gripper-evaluation pipelines under consistent
    conditions.

    \item \textbf{Evaluation preparation:}
    Integrate and adapt the problem-specific evaluation procedures required to assess candidate suction-cup layouts and rigid jaw contours.

    \item \textbf{Experimental design:}
    Define representative workpieces, predefined evaluation configurations, evaluation budgets, initialization conditions, repeated runs, and
    performance indicators for a controlled and reproducible comparison.

    \item \textbf{Algorithm application and comparison:}
    Apply selected evolutionary and Bayesian multi-objective optimization methods to both gripper-design problems and compare their performance using
    suitable multi-objective indicators.

    \item \textbf{Result analysis:}
    Analyze the obtained results with respect to solution quality, convergence behaviour, robustness across repeated runs, and sensitivity to the
    characteristics of the investigated gripper-design problems.
    
\end{itemize}
